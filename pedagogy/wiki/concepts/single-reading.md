# Single Reading

## Definition

[[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s companion rule to [[concepts/narration|narration]]: a passage, chapter, or lesson is read to the child (or, later, by the child) **once only**. The child attends knowing there will be no second chance. Mason treats this as a condition of the method, not a stylistic preference.

## Core Ideas

- **Attention is natural, not effortful.** Mason's striking claim — directly against the Victorian commonplace that children must be taught to attend: "Complete and entire attention is a natural function which requires no effort and causes no fatigue." What feels like effort is the mind wandering and being brought back, _not_ attention itself.
- **A second chance destroys the first.** If the child knows the passage will be re-read, the natural tendency is to save attention for later. Mason: "A _single reading_ is a condition insisted upon because a naturally desultory habit of mind leads us all to put off the effort of attention as long as a second or third chance of coping with our subject is to be hoped for."
- **The single reading is paired with the quality of the reading matter.** A [[concepts/living-books|living book]] _deserves_ one attentive reading; a textbook deserves none. The rule would be cruel with bad books.
- **The single reading is paired with the _kind_ of response required.** The child must immediately [[concepts/narration|narrate]]; the telling back is the guarantee of the attention.

## In Practice

- Young children: the teacher reads, paragraph by paragraph; each paragraph is narrated before the next is read.
- Older children (Form II onward): the teacher reads a chapter, or the child reads silently; narration follows at once.
- No explaining unfamiliar words unless the child asks. No illustrations to fill in. The author must be allowed to do his own work.
- In languages: nine pages of French read aloud, at ordinary speaking pace, followed immediately by narration in French. (Demonstrated repeatedly at the [[entities/house-of-education|House of Education]].)
- In terminal examinations: the child writes narrations on the term's reading without notes and without revision.

## Variants & Related Concepts

- [[concepts/narration|Narration]] — the response that single reading enables.
- [[concepts/living-books|Living Books]] — the material that makes single reading feasible.
- [[concepts/habit-of-attention|The Habit of Attention]] — single reading is the chief practical device for forming it: the child attends fully because no second chance is coming.
- [[concepts/education-is-atmosphere-discipline-life|Education is an Atmosphere, a Discipline, a Life]] — the habit of attention is the central "discipline."
- [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|Children are Born Persons]] — the child already possesses attention; the teacher's job is not to build it but not to sabotage it.

**Modern analogue:** Mason's single reading resembles the "desirable difficulty" tradition in learning research — making retrieval slightly harder in the moment yields more durable memory. Her mechanism (the prospect of re-reading destroys first-pass attention) is a testable hypothesis rather than an established finding.

## Key Proponents / Origins

- [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]] — the rule and the rationale are hers, though the underlying picture of attention as natural rather than effortful owes something to Henri Bergson (whom Mason cites on mind memory).

## Mentioned In

- [[sources/home-education|Home Education]] — Volume I (1906). Part V ("Reading for Older Children," "The Art of Narrating") states the rule directly: a child "should be trained from the first to think that one reading of any lesson is enough to enable him to narrate what he has read." If he cannot narrate after one reading, "let him not get the notion that he may, or that he must, read it again" — "a look of slight regret because there is a gap in his knowledge will convict him."
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book I]] — Principle 15, and the operating rule of Chapter X throughout.
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book II]] — Book II derives the single-reading rule directly from the [[concepts/act-of-knowing|act of knowing]]: the mind attends once _because_ knowing is a single definite act, not a repeated exposure.
