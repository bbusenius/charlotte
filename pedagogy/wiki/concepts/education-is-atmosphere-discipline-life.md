# Education is an Atmosphere, a Discipline, a Life

## Definition

[[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s triadic formula — **Principle 6** of the [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|20 Principles]] — for the three simultaneous and legitimate instruments of education. Anything that is not one of these three (suggestion, manipulation, coercion, mere drill) is illegitimate.

## Core Ideas

- **Atmosphere** — the environment the child actually lives in: the home, its books, its conversation, its moral climate. Crucially, Mason insists atmosphere is **not** a contrived child's world. She is scathing about nurseries, nursery language, children's furniture, and children's books written down to a supposed child's level. The child breathes the same air as the adult — and is the better for it.
- **Discipline** — the careful, patient formation of **habits**, above all habits of thought: the [[concepts/habit-of-attention|habit of attention]], the habit of obedience, the habit of good handwriting, the habit of truth-telling. Mason draws here on the long doctrine of [[concepts/habit-formation|habit formation]] worked out in [[sources/home-education|_Home Education_]]: a parent's or teacher's hardest job is to lay down the tramlines of habit early, because a formed habit carries the child where effort alone cannot.
- **Life** — the daily presentation of **living ideas**, delivered through [[concepts/living-books|living books]] and the events of the real world. "The mind feeds on ideas"; a child on a textbook-only diet is a child on bread and water.

The three are not stages; they operate together. A home with poor atmosphere will undo the best habit training. Good habits will not substitute for the absence of ideas. Ideas will not land in a mind formed without the habit of attention.

## In Practice

- **Atmosphere**: don't build a child's world — let the child into the world. Books, conversation, art, nature, religious practice at adult level. Parents are part of the curriculum.
- **Discipline**: the first educational task is to establish the habit of attention (hence [[concepts/single-reading|single reading]] — the child knows a second chance will not come). Other key habits: truthfulness, obedience, neatness.
- **Life**: a wide curriculum, many authors, many subjects, every day. Reading is primary; lectures and teacher-talk are secondary.

## Variants & Related Concepts

- [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|20 Principles of Education]] — this is Principle 6, with Principles 7–9 elaborating each term.
- [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|Children are Born Persons]] — atmosphere makes sense because the child is already a person at home in the adult world.
- [[concepts/living-books|Living Books]] — the material vehicle for "life."
- [[concepts/science-of-relations|Science of Relations]] — the curricular expression of "life": a wide field of potential relations to be opened.
- [[concepts/narration|Narration]] — the disciplined habit by which "life" (ideas) is metabolised into the person.

## Key Proponents / Origins

- [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]] — the triad is her coinage, though [[concepts/habit-formation|habit formation]] draws on Victorian associationist psychology (via [[entities/william-carpenter|Carpenter]]) and "atmosphere" has affinities with [[entities/friedrich-froebel|Froebel]] and the kindergarten tradition (which Mason nevertheless criticised for over-designing the child's environment).
