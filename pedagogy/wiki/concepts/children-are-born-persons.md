# Children are Born Persons

## Definition

[[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s cornerstone claim: every child arrives whole — possessing, in latent form, every mental, moral, imaginative, and spiritual faculty an adult has, and lacking only knowledge and experience. It is **Principle 1** of the [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|20 Principles of Education]] and the anthropological foundation on which her entire pedagogy rests.

## Core Ideas

- **Not a blank slate.** Mason rejects the Lockean _tabula rasa_: a child does not need to be written on so much as fed.
- **Not an animal to be trained.** She equally rejects the behaviourist / Herbartian picture of the child as a bundle of reflexes and "faculties" that must be coached into activity.
- **Not a miniature adult.** The difference from an adult is one of experience, not of essence.
- **Equality of essence across class.** If every child is a person, then no curriculum should be thinned or coarsened for working-class children. Mason cites the identical results of [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]] programmes in elementary Council schools and in fee-paying schools as proof.
- **The educator's stance is one of respect.** Because the child is already a person, the teacher withdraws to make room. Methods like [[concepts/narration|narration]] and [[concepts/single-reading|single reading]] are operational expressions of this respect: the child, not the teacher, does the intellectual work.

## In Practice

Almost every distinctive [[entities/charlotte-mason|Mason]] practice is downstream of this one claim:

- No "talking down" to children, no writing books "for the child level" — hence the insistence on [[concepts/living-books|living books]].
- No pre-digested textbook summaries: the child meets the author directly.
- No leading questions during narration, no interruption: the child's telling is his own.
- [[concepts/habit-formation|Habit formation]] rather than reward-and-punishment: habits shape the person the child already is.
- Terminal examinations trust the child's memory and judgment; revision is not offered.

## Variants & Related Concepts

- [[concepts/science-of-relations|Science of Relations]] — because the child is a person, he has potential relations to everything; education opens them.
- [[concepts/living-books|Living Books]] — what a person deserves to read.
- [[concepts/narration|Narration]] — what a person does when he knows something.
- [[concepts/way-of-the-will|Way of the Will]] — the person exercising self-command.
- [[concepts/education-is-atmosphere-discipline-life|Education is an Atmosphere, a Discipline, a Life]] — Principles 5–9, elaborating what a person needs to grow.

## Key Proponents / Origins

- [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]] — author of the doctrine in its distinctive form. Mason credits no single predecessor but invokes Wordsworth, Coleridge, and the Gospels in support; she opposes Herbart and the "faculty" psychologists of her day.

## Mentioned In

- [[sources/home-education|Home Education]] — Volume I (1906), the earliest statement in the wiki. Principle 1 of the synopsis, and the founding "central thought" of the book: "the somewhat obvious fact that the child is a _person_ with all the possibilities and powers included in personality." The child's estate is figured as "a prince committed to the fostering care of peasants," and the three Gospel laws — Offend not, Despise not, Hinder not — are framed as charges against the adult who fails the personality already present.
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book I]] — Principle 1; Chapter II bears the phrase as its title and elaborates the argument across several chapters.
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book II]] — Book II draws the social conclusion: if every child is a person, then no class of persons can be denied the full liberal curriculum. "Children are born persons" is the load-bearing premise of [[concepts/liberal-education-for-all|liberal education for all]].
