# The Act of Knowing

## Summary

[[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s epistemological claim, argued most fully in [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|Book II]]: _knowing_ is a definite, discoverable act of the mind, not a passive reception of information. It consists of the mind's attending to matter presented in literary form, grasping it, and rendering it back in the knower's own words. Until this act has been performed, the material has been "read," "heard," or "seen" but has not been _known_. [[concepts/narration|Narration]] is therefore not a teaching method among many but the test — and, indeed, the manifestation — of whether knowing has occurred.

## Core Claims

- Knowledge enters only through the [[concepts/knowledge-as-food|mind's feeding on ideas]], and the mind can feed only on matter that has literary character — the dry summary, the textbook, the question-and-answer catechism leave no trace.
- The act of knowing is complete only when the learner _tells_ what has been read or heard. Telling forces the mind to grasp, order, and hold.
- A [[concepts/single-reading|single reading]] is therefore sufficient and in fact preferable: the knowledge that survives one attentive exposure is the knowledge the mind has genuinely made its own; re-reading breeds inattention and the expectation that "we can go over it again."
- Because the act of knowing is the child's own, the teacher cannot perform it for him. The teacher's job is to choose the book and set the narration; the child does the knowing.
- Mason presents this not as novel theory but as the rediscovery of an ancient practice, corroborated (she claims) by Bergson's account of memory and attention.

## Connections

- The method that embodies the act of knowing is [[concepts/narration|narration]]
- The discipline that protects it is the [[concepts/single-reading|single reading]]
- The material it requires is [[concepts/living-books|living books]]
- It presupposes that [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|children are persons]] — i.e. agents, not vessels
- It underwrites the claim of [[concepts/liberal-education-for-all|liberal education for all]]: if knowing is a universal human act, no class of children is incapable of it
- It is the thing examinations test well when they ask "tell what you know of X" and test badly when they ask for the reproduction of teacher-supplied phrases (see [[concepts/too-wide-a-mesh|too wide a mesh]])
