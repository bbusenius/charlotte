# Charlotte Mason

**Type:** person (1842–1923)

## Summary

English educator, founder of the [[entities/pneu|Parents' National Educational Union]] (1887), the [[entities/parents-union-school|Parents' Union School]] (1891), and the [[entities/house-of-education|House of Education]] training college at Ambleside (1892). Her six-volume _Home Education Series_ — opening with [[sources/home-education|_Home Education_]] (1886) and culminating in [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Towards a Philosophy of Education_]] (1923) — remains the defining statement of a distinctive Anglophone pedagogy built around [[concepts/living-books|living books]], [[concepts/narration|narration]], the formation of [[concepts/habit-formation|habit]], and the conviction that [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|children are born persons]]. The wiki holds the first and last volumes of the Series, written some thirty-seven years apart.

## Key Contributions / Characteristics

- Formulated the [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|20 Principles of Education]], the compact creed that has since travelled as a stand-alone document through the homeschool world; it heads the 1923 _Philosophy of Education_ in its final twenty-point form, having appeared as an **eighteen-point** synopsis in _Home Education_ as early as 1906.
- Treated [[concepts/narration|oral and written narration]] after a [[concepts/single-reading|single attentive reading]] as the universal method for every subject — replacing textbooks, comprehension questions, and rote memorisation.
- Insisted on a wide, generous curriculum for children of every class; her programmes ran identically in fee-paying schools, home schoolrooms, and Gloucestershire Council (state) schools under HMI [[entities/house-of-education|H. W. Household]]'s patronage.
- Wrote in opposition to the Herbartian "faculties" psychology dominant in her day: rejected the idea that memory, reason, imagination must each be separately "trained," holding instead that a mind fed with ideas exercises all its powers naturally.
- Framed education as the formation of [[concepts/science-of-relations|relations]] — with persons, nature, art, country, God, past, and present — rather than the accumulation of skills or facts.
- Grounded education in a doctrine of [[concepts/habit-formation|habit formation]] — "the formation of habits _is_ education" — backed by the brain physiology of [[entities/william-carpenter|Dr Carpenter]]; the [[concepts/habit-of-attention|habit of attention]] she held to be "the primary object of all mental discipline."
- Made [[concepts/nature-study|out-of-door life and nature study]] the core of education before age nine — long days outside, first-hand observation, the nature note-book — paired with a doctrine of [[concepts/masterly-inactivity|masterly inactivity]], the wise letting-alone that gives a child room to grow.
- Drew a foundational distinction between [[concepts/method-vs-system|a method and a system]] of education, and worked her principles down to the level of concrete lesson method — how to teach reading, spelling, arithmetic, history, geography.
- A long career: the lectures behind _Home Education_ were given in 1885, the final synthesis published in 1923. The conceptual spine — persons, narration, single reading, living books, the science of relations, the Way of the Will, habit — is already complete in the first book.
- Died in 1923, the year _Philosophy of Education_ was published; the book is effectively her valedictory.

## Connections

- Founded [[entities/pneu|PNEU]], [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]], and [[entities/house-of-education|House of Education]]
- Principal proponent of [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|Children are Born Persons]], [[concepts/education-is-atmosphere-discipline-life|Education is an Atmosphere, a Discipline, a Life]], [[concepts/science-of-relations|Science of Relations]], [[concepts/narration|Narration]], [[concepts/single-reading|Single Reading]], [[concepts/living-books|Living Books]], [[concepts/way-of-the-will|Way of the Will]], [[concepts/knowledge-of-god-man-universe|Knowledge of God, Man, Universe]], [[concepts/habit-formation|Habit Formation]], [[concepts/habit-of-attention|the Habit of Attention]], [[concepts/nature-study|Nature Study]], [[concepts/masterly-inactivity|Masterly Inactivity]], [[concepts/method-vs-system|Method versus System]]
- Cites approvingly: [[entities/william-carpenter|Carpenter]] (the physiology of habit), Bergson (on mind memory), Wordsworth, Coleridge, Ruskin, Plato, Montaigne, [[entities/plutarch|Plutarch]], Comenius, Pestalozzi, Herbert Spencer
- Cites critically: [[entities/johann-herbart|Herbart]], Karl Marx
- Honours but criticises: [[entities/friedrich-froebel|Froebel]] and the Kindergarten — a revered predecessor whose system she nonetheless judges over-organised
- Notes as a parallel movement: Grundtvig's Danish folk high schools

## Mentioned In

- [[sources/home-education|Home Education]] — author; Volume I of her Series and her first book (1886; the ingested text is the fifth edition, 1906). It is the foundation of the whole system and the most operationally detailed of her works — concrete methods for the education of children under nine — and shows the conceptual spine already complete decades before the 1923 synthesis.
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book I]] — author; the book is her most mature and most compact statement of the whole philosophy.
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book II]] — author; Book II turns the philosophy of Book I outward to argue for [[concepts/liberal-education-for-all|a liberal education for all]], section by section across Elementary, Secondary, and Continuation Schools, and closes with the "Too Wide a Mesh" critique of the exam system.
