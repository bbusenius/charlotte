# Johann Friedrich Herbart

**Type:** person (German philosopher, psychologist, and pedagogue, 1776–1841)

## Summary

The German philosopher who did more than anyone to establish pedagogy as a systematic academic discipline, and whose psychology — transmitted into late-Victorian teaching practice as **"the Herbartian doctrine"** — is the named theoretical antithesis of [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s account of the mind. Where Mason engages [[entities/friedrich-froebel|Froebel]] as a revered predecessor, she engages Herbart as the position she is arguing _against_.

## Mason's Critique

- **The mind is not a receptacle.** The Herbartian picture, as Mason states it, is of the mind as a store into which ideas are "dropped, each idea adding to an 'apperception mass' of its like." Synopsis point 9 of _Home Education_ rejects this directly; point 10 offers the counter-image — "a child's mind is no mere _sac_ to hold ideas; but is rather... a spiritual _organism_, with an appetite for all knowledge."
- **The doctrine of interest puts the stress in the wrong place.** "The Herbartian doctrine lays the stress of education — the preparation of knowledge in enticing morsels, presented in due order — upon the teacher." The consequence Mason fears: "Children taught upon this principle are in danger of receiving much teaching with little knowledge."
- **"What a child learns matters less than how he learns it."** Mason quotes this as the Herbartian teacher's axiom and rejects it: because the normal child has "powers of mind that fit him to deal with all knowledge proper to him," the proper response is a full and generous curriculum of vital ideas, not pre-digested "morsels."
- The contrast is the seed of [[concepts/knowledge-as-food|knowledge as the food of the mind]]: a mind that is an organism with an appetite, not a sac to be filled.

## Connections

- The named foil for synopsis points 9–11 of [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|Mason's principles]] — the 1906 _Home Education_ list is noticeably more polemical against Herbart than the 1923 twenty-principle version.
- Named in the _Home Education_ Preface alongside [[entities/friedrich-froebel|Froebel]] as one of the incomplete philosophies educators "fall back upon."
- Mason's later [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Towards a Philosophy of Education_]] (1923) also mentions Herbart dismissively; the substantive engagement, however, is in _Home Education_.
