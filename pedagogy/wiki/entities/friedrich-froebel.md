# Friedrich Froebel

**Type:** person (German educator, 1782–1852; founder of the Kindergarten)

## Summary

The German educator who founded the **Kindergarten** — a designed environment of "gifts" (manipulable objects) and "occupations" (structured activities) for the education of young children, named for the idea of a garden in which children grow. In _Home Education_ [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]] treats Froebel as a revered predecessor whose system she has nonetheless outgrown: she devotes two chapters of Part V to honouring his insight and dismantling his method.

## Mason's Engagement

- **Reverence.** "We reverence Froebel." Mason credits him above all with one thing: "He raised an altar to the enthusiasm of childhood upon which the flame has never since gone out." Many of his great thoughts — the child's relations to the universe — she regards as shared rather than borrowed, "at least as old as Plato"; his achievement was to gather "diffused thought and practice into a system."
- **The "false analogy" of the garden.** Mason's central objection: "the child became a plant in a well-ordered garden." A plant may rightly be potted, pruned, and trained to its owner's purpose, but "_persons_ do not grow in a garden." To figure a [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|person]] by any analogy is "dangerous and misleading."
- **A method that decays into a system.** The Kindergarten, as Froebel conceived it, "deserves the name" of a [[concepts/method-vs-system|method]] — but in "the hands of ignorant practitioners" the gifts and games become "so many instruments of _wooden_ teaching."
- **Specific criticisms.** The "mother-games" are "too strenuous for a child"; the society of many equals of one age is over-stimulating; the teacher's "personal magnetism" overrides the child's own initiative; the elaborate organisation risks **supplanting Nature** where it means only to supplement her. "Teachers mediate too much."
- **The mother as the true _Kindergärtnerin_.** Mason keeps Froebel's _principles_ (the senses trained, the child's joyousness honoured, perfect execution expected) while discarding the Kindergarten _practices_; the home, with the mother, is the better "growing-place."

## Connections

- The figure against whom Mason defines [[concepts/masterly-inactivity|masterly inactivity]] (the over-organised environment as the danger) and [[concepts/method-vs-system|method versus system]] (the Kindergarten as a method decayed into a system).
- "Atmosphere" in [[concepts/education-is-atmosphere-discipline-life|Education is an Atmosphere, a Discipline, a Life]] has affinities with the Kindergarten tradition — which Mason nonetheless criticises for over-designing the child's environment.
- Named in the _Home Education_ Preface alongside [[entities/johann-herbart|Herbart]] as one of the part-philosophies educators "fall back upon" for want of a complete one.
