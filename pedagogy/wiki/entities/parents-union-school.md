# Parents' Union School (PUS)

**Type:** organization (a network / curriculum programme, not a single building)

## Summary

The school programme run by the [[entities/pneu|PNEU]] from 1891 onwards: a centrally-designed termly curriculum, reading list, and examination, delivered in affiliated homes, private schools, and elementary Council schools. The PUS is the *operational* form of [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s philosophy — the body of classroom practice from which [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Towards a Philosophy of Education_]] draws every example, quote, and examination answer.

## Key Contributions / Characteristics

- Organised into **Forms** by age: Form I (6–9), Form II (9–12), Form III (12–13), Form IV (13–15), Forms V and VI (15–18), with parallel A/B divisions.
- A single programme each term specified books and questions for _every_ subject — history (English, French, ancient, general), literature (with contemporary Shakespeare or Scott), Bible, citizenship (Plutarch), composition (oral and written), languages (French, Latin, German, Italian), art ("picture study" of a single artist's work), musical appreciation, sciences (natural history, physiology, physics), geography, mathematics, handicrafts.
- Method, uniform across the programme: a [[concepts/single-reading|single reading]] of a [[concepts/living-books|living book]] followed by [[concepts/narration|narration]]; no re-reading, no comprehension questions during narration, no rote memorisation.
- Examinations at the end of each term: children answered — without revision — questions covering the whole programme. The unrevised examination is Mason's central evidence that the method produces durable knowledge rather than examination-hall cramming.

## Connections

- Run by the [[entities/pneu|PNEU]]
- Founded by [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]
- Teachers largely trained at the [[entities/house-of-education|House of Education]]
- The programmes implement [[concepts/science-of-relations|the science of relations]] by exposing every child to the widest possible range of subjects and authors.

## Mentioned In

- [[sources/home-education|Home Education]] — Volume I (1906). Appendices C and D reproduce actual PUS-style termly programmes and examination papers — "Class IB" (a child of seven) and "Class II" (a child of nine) — listing the term's books and questions for Bible, recitation, sums, writing, reading, English history, tales, natural history, picture talk, French, geography, and handicrafts, with specimen answers. They are the earliest concrete PUS programme in the wiki.
