# Pedagogy Wiki — Overview

_Last updated: 2026-05-22 | Sources ingested: 4_

## What This Wiki Is

A personal knowledge base about pedagogy, homeschooling theory, educational methods, and learning science. Built using the [[concepts/llm-wiki-pattern]] — an LLM maintains all wiki files while the human curates sources and asks questions.

## Current State

The wiki is centered on [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]], now represented across her career rather than at its end alone: [[sources/home-education|_Home Education_]] (Volume I of her _Home Education Series_; 1885 lectures, fifth edition 1906) and [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Towards a Philosophy of Education_]], Books [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|I]] and [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|II]] (Volume VI; 1923). Together they give a doctrine of the person, a doctrine of knowledge, a tight method (single reading + narration + living books), a doctrine of habit, a programme of out-of-door life, and a social thesis ("a liberal education for all") — the first volume practical and early-childhood-focused, the last theoretical and society-facing.

The wiki also holds one meta-structural source — [[sources/karpathy-llm-wiki]] — which describes this wiki's own pattern and is not part of the pedagogy domain.

## Emerging Themes

### The Charlotte Mason skeleton (now three volumes, spanning a career)

Mason's system — all of it downstream of a short list of principles — provides the large majority of the wiki's concept pages. The corpus now spans nearly four decades: [[sources/home-education|_Home Education_]] (Volume I, from 1885 lectures) and [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Philosophy of Education_]] (Volume VI, 1923). The decisive thing the first volume shows is that the skeleton below was **already in place at the start** — the 1923 books refined and systematised a structure Mason had been teaching since the 1880s. (One small correction follows: the prefatory creed had **eighteen** numbered principles in 1906, not twenty — see [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|20 Principles]].)

- **The person-centred anthropology.** [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|Children are born persons]]: whole, equipped with every adult faculty, lacking only knowledge and experience. Almost every distinctive Mason practice is downstream of this claim.
- **The triadic instrument.** [[concepts/education-is-atmosphere-discipline-life|Education is an atmosphere, a discipline, and a life]]: the home/world the child actually lives in, the formation of habits (above all the habit of attention), and the daily ration of living ideas — operating together, not in sequence.
- **The curricular orientation.** [[concepts/science-of-relations|Education is the science of relations]]: schooling opens the child's latent relations to persons, nature, art, country, past, present, and God. Hence breadth over depth in the early years, and the deliberately unnarrowed [[concepts/knowledge-of-god-man-universe|threefold curriculum]] — which Book II now traces explicitly to [[entities/matthew-arnold|Matthew Arnold]]'s classification (Divinity, Humanities, Science).
- **The method.** [[concepts/single-reading|A single attentive reading]] of a [[concepts/living-books|living book]], followed by [[concepts/narration|narration]] by the child. This is the operational core: no re-reading, no comprehension questions, no pre-digested summaries, no textbook-and-worksheet apparatus. Book II recasts the method as the visible form of the underlying [[concepts/act-of-knowing|act of knowing]].
- **The moral theory.** [[concepts/way-of-the-will|The Way of the Will]] — volition as redirection of attention rather than direct effort, and reason as a servant that demonstrates whatever premise the will accepts. Book II pushes this into politics: whole nations act on whatever demonstrations their reason makes from whatever premises their will has been fed.

### What Home Education adds (the early years, the body, and habit)

[[sources/home-education|_Home Education_]] is concerned specifically with **children under nine**, and it carries three large bodies of material the 1923-sourced pages do not.

- **A physiological floor.** Before anything else, Mason grounds education in the body: the brain needs well-oxygenated blood, varied work, rest, fresh air. "Intellectual, moral, even spiritual life and progress depend greatly upon physical conditions." This is "the lowest round of the educational ladder" — and the necessary one.
- **[[concepts/habit-formation|Habit]] as the working instrument.** "The formation of habits _is_ education." Where the 1923 triad names "the discipline of habit" in a phrase, _Home Education_ devotes two Parts to it: the physiology (drawn from [[entities/william-carpenter|Dr Carpenter]]), the worked method ("Shut the door after you"), the "dangerous stage" at which a half-formed habit is lost, and the supreme habit — [[concepts/habit-of-attention|attention]], trained by short, varied, timed lessons.
- **[[concepts/nature-study|Out-of-door life]] and [[concepts/masterly-inactivity|masterly inactivity]].** The young child's first education is first-hand acquaintance with nature — long days outside, the nature note-book, the child as naturalist — and much of the educator's work is _wise letting-alone_, the active restraint that leaves a child "an inch of space, wherein to wonder — and grow."

It is also, for the practical purpose of the homeschool-hub authoring skills, the most usable Mason source: it descends from principle to concrete lesson method — reading by loose-word boxes, spelling by visualising the word and prepared dictation, arithmetic with beans, history through one chronicle of one period, geography through the traveller and the map. And it draws the foundational distinction — [[concepts/method-vs-system|a method, not a system]] — that frames everything else.

### The social dimension (new in Book II)

Book II opens the case out from the schoolroom to the nation and the class system.

- **[[concepts/liberal-education-for-all|Liberal education for all]]** is the explicit thesis: the same wide, literary, humane curriculum for every child — in Council schools as in private ones. The slogan Mason borrows is [[entities/comenius|Comenius]]'s _omnia omnibus omnino_. The empirical ground is thirty years of [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]] results across social classes.
- **The antithesis is the utilitarian Continuation School** (Kerschensteiner's Munich model). Mason's alternative model is [[entities/nfs-grundtvig|Grundtvig]]'s Danish People's High Schools — humanistic, literary, Christian, national — but explicitly reformed against Grundtvig's pessimistic concession that "all is not for all."
- **[[concepts/knowledge-as-food|Knowledge is the food of the mind]]** — the Book II metaphor that joins the anthropology (everyone has a mind that needs feeding) to the curriculum (the food must be _real_, in literary form) to the social claim (no class of child should have the nutrition withheld).
- **[[concepts/too-wide-a-mesh|Too wide a mesh]]** — Mason's closing critique of the State examination system. As long as the net catches only what can be crammed, the schools will teach only what can be crammed. Reform the exam or the curriculum will not move.

### Stance pending further sources

The pedagogy corpus is still narrow. The wiki has not yet:

- Measured Mason against modern cognitive science (Mason's "single attentive reading + narration" is clearly a form of retrieval practice but with no notion of spacing or interleaving).
- Read Mason's interlocutors and contemporaries from _their_ own side. _Home Education_ now brings [[entities/friedrich-froebel|Froebel]] (a revered predecessor) and [[entities/johann-herbart|Herbart]] (the named antithesis) into the wiki — but only as Mason reports them; their own writings, and Montessori, Dewey, and Steiner, remain un-ingested, as does the broader American homeschool-curriculum market (Sonlight, My Father's World, Memoria Press, Classical Conversations, Abeka, Bob Jones, Ambleside Online, Simply Charlotte Mason).
- Ingested the four middle volumes of Mason's own series — _Parents and Children_ (II), _School Education_ (III), _Ourselves_ (IV), and _Some Studies in the Formation of Character_ (V). The wiki holds only the first volume and the last; _School Education_ in particular carries the chapter-length treatment of [[concepts/masterly-inactivity|masterly inactivity]] that Volume I only names.
- Surveyed critical literature on Mason — either her contemporary reception or the 20th- and 21st-century homeschool revival that has canonised her in the anglophone homeschool world.
- Corroborated Mason's striking empirical claim that [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]] programmes worked identically across social classes (Book II repeats the claim but adds no independent documentation).
- Examined [[entities/nfs-grundtvig|Grundtvig]]'s Danish model or the Kerschensteiner Munich model against their own primary sources.

These are natural next ingests.

## Key Open Questions

> [!question] How does Mason's "single reading + narration" method hold up against modern cognitive-science findings on retrieval practice, spacing, and interleaving? On its face narration is retrieval practice — but without the spacing.

> [!question] Is Mason's claim that PUS programmes produced equivalent results in Council elementary schools and elite private schools corroborated by independent records? The Gloucestershire experiment under H. W. Household is the specific case she cites, and Book II makes it a load-bearing claim.

> [!question] What pedagogical frameworks are most relevant to a homeschool context vs. an institutional one?

> [!question] How does Mason's pedagogy map onto — and differ from — Montessori, Dewey, Steiner, and later unschooling traditions?

> [!question] Mason treats Bible study as literary study (single reading, narration, no hortatory comment). Is her method religiously serious because it is methodologically literary, or the other way around?

> [!question] Book II's [[concepts/too-wide-a-mesh|Too Wide a Mesh]] diagnosis — that examinations shape curricula downward — is a hundred years old. Is it borne out by the 20th- and 21st-century history of standardised testing?

> [!question] Mason's prefatory creed grew from **eighteen** principles in the 1906 [[sources/home-education|_Home Education_]] to **twenty** in the 1923 [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Philosophy of Education_]]. What changed between them, and when? The "20 Principles" circulate in the homeschool world as a fixed, timeless document; the wiki can now show they were neither. Tracing the evolution would require the four un-ingested middle volumes of the series.

> [!question] Mason's doctrine of [[concepts/habit-formation|habit]] rests on [[entities/william-carpenter|Carpenter]]'s Victorian _Mental Physiology_ — repeated thought wearing a "rut" in growing brain tissue. The behavioural claims have largely held up; the mechanism is period science. How much of Mason's confidence in habit-training (and her near-deterministic "Habit is ten natures") survives if the physiology is read only as metaphor?
