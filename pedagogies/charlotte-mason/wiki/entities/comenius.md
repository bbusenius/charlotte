# Comenius

**Type:** person (Jan Amos Komenský, 1592–1670, Czech pedagogue and bishop of the Unity of the Brethren)

## Summary

Seventeenth-century Moravian educator, author of the _Didactica Magna_ and the _Orbis Sensualium Pictus_, and originator of the slogan [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]] adopts as the motto of her social thesis: **"All knowledge for all men"** (or, in Comenius's Latin ideal, _omnia omnibus omnino_ — everything to all, altogether). Mason cites this phrase in [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|Book II]] Ch. III as the hope she believes the [[entities/parents-union-school|Parents' Union School]] programmes have made practically realisable.

## Key Contributions / Characteristics

- Advocated universal, lifelong, methodical education — anticipating by centuries both compulsory schooling and the democratic premise that a literary education is the right of every child.
- Formulated the pedagogical ideal that knowledge be presented to all, of every class, in their own language and with their own understanding — the phrase Mason claims to have fulfilled in practice.
- Mason places Comenius alongside Plato and Erasmus as a forerunner whose theoretical ideal she now believes demonstrated in concrete PUS results.

## Connections

- The motto "All knowledge for all men" is the banner under which Mason's [[concepts/liberal-education-for-all|liberal-education-for-all]] thesis marches.
- Named in Book I's list of admired predecessors (Plato, Montaigne, Comenius, Erasmus).
- Anticipates the modern Czech national-educational tradition; Comenius is honoured as a national figure in the Czech Republic.
