# Plutarch

**Type:** person (Greek biographer and Platonist philosopher, c. 46 – after 119 CE)

## Summary

The author of the _Parallel Lives_ (the _Bioi Paralleloi_, usually called _Plutarch's Lives_); [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s "great authority" for the teaching of citizenship and moral reflection in the [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]] curriculum. Mason's schools read Plutarch in Sir Thomas North's Elizabethan translation; children aged 10–14 narrate full Lives, and examination papers include verses written in response to them.

## Key Contributions / Characteristics

- _Parallel Lives_ — paired biographies of Greek and Roman statesmen (Pericles/Fabius, Alcibiades/Coriolanus, etc.), read in PUS as the spine of Mason's citizenship curriculum.
- Read in **North's 1579 English translation** (itself the direct source of Shakespeare's Roman plays). Mason treats both works — North's Plutarch and Shakespeare's Romans — as a paired programme.
- Intended by Mason to give children the "ethical" dimension of history directly, through the moral life of great men, rather than through moralising by the teacher.
- Quoted by Mason in Book II: "[Plutarch] hath written the profitable story of all authors. … this man being excellent in wit, learning, and experience, hath chosen the special acts of the best persons, of the famousest nations of the world." (From North's preface.)
- In PUS practice, a child of 14 narrates the _Life of Pericles_ in verse as part of a terminal examination — Book II reproduces one such specimen.
