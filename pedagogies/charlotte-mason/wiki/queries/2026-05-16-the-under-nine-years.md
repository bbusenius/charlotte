# The Under-Nine Years

"The under-nine years" is not a sub-topic of [[sources/home-education|_Home Education_]] — it is the **entire scope of the book**. The subtitle is _The Education of Children under Nine Years of Age_, and [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s Preface to the Fourth Edition states that the volume "attempts to deal with the whole of education from infancy until the ninth year of life." Mason treats infancy-to-nine as a single developmental block, but works it in two distinct phases.

## Phase 1 — birth to six: "a quiet growing time"

Mason: "perhaps a mother's first duty to her children is to secure for them a quiet growing time, a full six years of passive receptive life." There are **no formal lessons** in this phase. The child's "business in the world during the first six or seven years of his life" is "to find out all he can, about whatever comes under his notice, by means of his five senses." Mason calls this "the storing time," spent "laying up images of things familiar" on which all later thought will draw. In practice the phase is built from:

- **[[concepts/nature-study|Out-of-door life and nature study]]** — four to six hours daily in the open; first-hand acquaintance with plants, trees, and creatures; the nature note-book. This is the young child's first and most important education.
- **Habit caught from the home atmosphere** — cleanliness, order, regularity, and courtesy are "received passively," imbibed "as the air he breathes" (see [[concepts/habit-formation|habit formation]]).
- **[[concepts/masterly-inactivity|Masterly inactivity]]** — wise, watchful letting-alone; the child is left "an inch of space, wherein to wonder — and grow."
- **A physiological floor** — a healthy, well-nourished brain, fresh air, rest, sleep; "the lowest round of the educational ladder," and the necessary one.

Mason's summary rule for the phase: "Let the child lie fallow till he is six."

## Phase 2 — six to nine: definite lessons begin

"At six or seven, definite lessons should begin." Mason holds that "the period of a child's life between his sixth and his ninth year should be used to lay the basis of a liberal education, and of the _habit_ of reading for instruction." Part V of _Home Education_ is, in her words, "a fairly complete introduction to methods of teaching subjects fit for children between the ages of six and nine." In this phase:

- Lessons are **short (ten to twenty minutes) and deliberately varied** — the chief device for forming the [[concepts/habit-of-attention|habit of attention]].
- **Reading is taught**, through Mason's whole-word-plus-word-building method (the "Cock Robin" and "Little Pussy" loose-word boxes).
- **[[concepts/narration|Narration]] begins at six.** Before six the child narrates "only when and what he has a mind to"; from six he tells back the fairy tale, the animal story, or the Bible passage, heard once.
- The **will, the conscience, and "the divine life"** begin to be deliberately, if sparingly, cultivated.

## Why nine

Nine is a threshold, not an arbitrary stopping point. "The child of eight or nine is able to tackle the more serious material of knowledge," and _Home Education_ deliberately confines itself to "what children under nine can narrate." In Mason's later [[entities/parents-union-school|Parents' Union School]] scheme this block corresponds to **Form I** (ages 6–9). _Home Education_ is therefore, in effect, the manual for the pre-school years together with Form I.

## Why this matters for the wiki

The wiki's other Mason source — the 1923 [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Towards a Philosophy of Education_]] — covers the whole PUS span (Forms I–VI, ages 6–18) at the level of principle. _Home Education_ is specifically the **foundation**: it is the only source in the wiki that treats infancy and early childhood, and the only one carrying concrete lesson methods. Four concept pages — [[concepts/nature-study|nature study]], [[concepts/habit-formation|habit formation]], [[concepts/habit-of-attention|the habit of attention]], and [[concepts/masterly-inactivity|masterly inactivity]] — together describe almost the whole of what Mason wants for a child before six.
