# Knowledge as the Food of the Mind

## Summary

[[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s governing metaphor for how a child's mind grows. The mind requires knowledge the way the body requires food: as a daily, varied, real intake that is _consumed_ and _assimilated_, not merely rehearsed or tested. Book II develops the metaphor into a practical rule — that what is set before children must be of nutritive quality ([[concepts/living-books|living books]], literary ideas, real information) rather than "twaddle," predigested summaries, or utilitarian reading-schemes that have had the nourishment squeezed out of them.

## Core Claims

- Mind is sustained on _ideas_, not on facts-in-the-abstract. An idea lives in literary form; stripped of its literary dress it becomes the gruel of the textbook and the mind starves on it.
- The metaphor governs curriculum choice: you do not feed a child on summaries any more than you feed him on capsules. A child needs Plutarch himself, not a "Plutarch for children."
- The metaphor governs method: the child's mind, like his body, does the assimilating. The teacher sets the table; the child digests. Hence [[concepts/narration|narration]] rather than comprehension questions.
- It governs breadth: the mind, like the body, thrives on varied diet. Mason's [[concepts/knowledge-of-god-man-universe|threefold curriculum]] — Divinity, Humanities, Science — is not an arbitrary inventory but the three food-groups a human mind needs.
- It governs quantity: too _little_ matter bores the mind; too _much_ spoils the appetite. Hence the [[concepts/single-reading|single reading]] and the discipline of finishing a book rather than endlessly re-visiting short passages.

## Connections

- The metaphor picks out what a [[concepts/living-books|living book]] is — nutritive prose, not the predigested textbook
- It motivates the [[concepts/act-of-knowing|act of knowing]] through [[concepts/narration|narration]]: digestion, not storage
- It grounds [[concepts/liberal-education-for-all|liberal education for all]]: every child's mind needs the same food; class cannot alter metabolism
- Explicitly anti-Herbartian: Mason rejects the faculty-psychology picture of separate mental "muscles" each needing separate training
- Closely related to the [[concepts/science-of-relations|science of relations]] — knowledge is food _because_ it forms living relationships in the knower

## Mentioned In

- [[sources/home-education|Home Education]] — Volume I (1906); the wiki's earliest statement of the metaphor. Synopsis points 8 and 10: "The mind feeds on ideas," and the mind is "a spiritual _organism_, with an appetite for all knowledge... which it can digest and assimilate as the body does foodstuffs." Part V develops it: children "learn, to grow," on "the mental _pabulum_ of assimilated knowledge"; an idea is "a spiritual germ endowed with vital force"; and "a morning in which a child receives no new idea is a morning wasted."
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book I]] — the metaphor underlies all of Mason's curricular argument.
- [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|Towards a Philosophy of Education — Book II]] — Ch. I and Ch. IV.II state the metaphor explicitly and derive concrete curricular rules from it: nothing but real matter, always in literary form, always narrated.
