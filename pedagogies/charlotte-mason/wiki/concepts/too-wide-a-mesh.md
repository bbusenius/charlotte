# Too Wide a Mesh

## Summary

[[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s image, and the title of the Supplementary chapter of [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-2|Book II]], for what is wrong with the English examination system. A national exam, she says, is a net; and the net currently in use has a mesh so wide that the things most worth keeping — a child's growing acquaintance with persons, places, ideas, literature, moral imagination — fall through it. What the mesh _does_ catch is what can be measured in short-answer form: dates, definitions, the teacher's phrasing, examiner-pleasing formulae.

## Core Claims

- Teaching follows testing. If the exam catches only the coarse matter, the teaching will aim only at the coarse matter, and the fine matter — the matter the child actually needs — will cease to be taught.
- The PUS examinations (unrevised, terminal, "tell what you know of X") are the rival model. They ask the child to narrate, in her own words, from the whole term's reading. They catch what the net with a wider mesh cannot: continuous thought, literary voice, formed judgement.
- The wider-mesh nets the State uses are not neutral instruments; they shape the schools and the children alike, and they shape them _downward_ — toward cramming, toward teacher-supplied phrasing, toward the forgetting that follows examination day.
- Reform of examinations, not reform of syllabi, is therefore the lever. As long as the mesh is wide, the syllabus will drift back to whatever the mesh catches.
- The remedy is not _abolishing_ exams but narrowing the mesh: examinations that ask the child to draw freely on what he has read, in continuous prose, and whose answers cannot be crammed for.

## Connections

- The practical rival is the [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]] terminal examination, built on [[concepts/narration|narration]]
- The diagnosis assumes the [[concepts/act-of-knowing|act of knowing]] — what a real examination should catch is the trace of that act
- The diagnosis applies the [[concepts/knowledge-as-food|knowledge-as-food]] metaphor: the wide-mesh exam rewards regurgitation, which is not digestion
- It is a companion critique to Mason's argument against the utilitarian Continuation School (Kerschensteiner); both chapters are about institutional designs that shape children downward
- Part of the broader case for [[concepts/liberal-education-for-all|liberal education for all]]: the current exam system fails working-class children most of all because it tests the most easily mimicked things
