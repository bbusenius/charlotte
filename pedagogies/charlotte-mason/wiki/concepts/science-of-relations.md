# Education is the Science of Relations

## Definition

[[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s master curricular idea — **Principle 12** of the [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|20 Principles]]: the child is born with latent relations to an enormous range of things — persons, nature, art, country, past, present, mathematics, language, God — and the work of education is to _open and preserve_ those relations, not to accumulate isolated facts or drill isolated skills.

## Core Ideas

- A **relation** in Mason's sense is a living interest: a felt connection between the child and some portion of the world that, once established, continues to grow without the teacher's continuing intervention.
- The curriculum exists to **expose** the child to as many fields as possible, because one cannot predict which relations will take root in which person. Hence the remarkably wide [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]] programme: Bible, history (English, French, ancient, general), literature, citizenship (Plutarch), composition, two or three languages, art, music, natural history, physics, physiology, geography, mathematics, handicrafts — every term, for every child.
- The curriculum is **not** to be narrowed for "practical" ends (poor children shall not have a poor diet) nor for specialism (the future mathematician shall still meet Shakespeare).
- This commits Mason to a distinctive view of knowledge: facts are not the unit. The unit is the relation, and the relation is carried by persons — authors, historical figures, artists — speaking directly to the child through [[concepts/living-books|living books]].

## In Practice

- Breadth over depth in the early Forms: better to have met many authors once than to have mastered one textbook.
- Every subject is taught through books of literary merit, not through teacher-compiled notes.
- History is chronological and simultaneous across countries (English, French, ancient) — to avoid the "insular and arrogant habit of mind" of single-nation study.
- Art is studied via "picture study" — six reproductions of one artist per term, examined in silence then narrated — so that a relation is formed with the artist.
- Music, similarly, by listening with attention: "musical appreciation" as distinct from instrument practice.
- Geography is "panoramic" rather than inferential: a region unfolded as a landscape the child inhabits imaginatively.

## Variants & Related Concepts

- [[concepts/20-principles-of-education|20 Principles of Education]] — Principle 12, the master idea.
- [[concepts/children-are-born-persons|Children are Born Persons]] — the relations are there because the child is already a person who can be related to things.
- [[concepts/living-books|Living Books]] — the vehicle of relations (a textbook cannot be related to).
- [[concepts/nature-study|Nature Study]] — the science of relations applied to the youngest children: opening the child's relation with the natural world at first hand.
- [[concepts/knowledge-of-god-man-universe|Knowledge of God, of Man, of the Universe]] — the three great domains in which relations are sought.
- [[concepts/education-is-atmosphere-discipline-life|Education is an Atmosphere, a Discipline, a Life]] — "life" is the ration of ideas by which relations are fed.

## Key Proponents / Origins

- [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]] — the phrase is hers. The underlying picture has affinities with Newman's _Idea of a University_ (knowledge as a connected whole) and with Ruskin's aesthetics, both of whom Mason read closely.
