# Knowledge of God, of Man, of the Universe

## Definition

The threefold division of the curriculum in [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s [[entities/parents-union-school|PUS]] programmes, expounded in Chapter X of [[sources/towards-a-philosophy-of-education-book-1|_Towards a Philosophy of Education_]]: every subject taught in the school fits under one of three headings — **Knowledge of God** (Bible, religion), **Knowledge of Man** (history, literature, citizenship, composition, languages, art, music), or **Knowledge of the Universe** (natural history, science, geography, mathematics, handicrafts).

## Core Ideas

- The division is Mason's practical scheme for realising the broader doctrine that [[concepts/science-of-relations|education is the science of relations]]: a full curriculum must open relations in all three great domains of knowledge.
- **Knowledge of God** is treated, methodologically, like every other subject: reverent reading of the Bible text (with scholarly introduction), followed by [[concepts/narration|narration]]. Dogmatic and hortatory teaching are avoided; Mason trusts the text to do its own work.
- **Knowledge of Man** is the bulk of the programme. History is chronological and *simultaneous across countries* (English + French + ancient, read in parallel) to avoid insularity. Literature runs _pari passu_ with history. Citizenship begins with Plutarch's _Lives_ — read unbowdlerised but with "suitable omissions" — and moves on to British constitutional questions and contemporary politics. Composition is not taught directly; it develops out of years of narration. Languages (French, Latin, German, Italian) are taught by the same single-reading-and-narration method, including in the target language. Art is taught by "picture study" — six reproductions of a single artist per term, examined in silence then narrated. Music is taught by listening, as "musical appreciation."
- **Knowledge of the Universe** covers natural history (field study, nature notebooks, bird lists, plant lists, "Book of Centuries" for artefacts), the sciences (physics, physiology, biology, geology) through literary treatments, geography by the "panoramic" method (region-by-region, history and industry intertwined, not by inferential principles alone), and mathematics — studied, Mason insists, for its own beauty but not permitted to dominate the curriculum.

## In Practice

- Mason is emphatic that a child should meet _all three_ domains every term from Form I to Form VI. Specialism can come later.
- Even working-class Council-school children followed the full tripartite programme: this is the concrete form of Mason's claim that "a liberal education is the right of every child."
- The threefold scheme is implicit in the textual layout of Chapter X, whose three main sections — Knowledge of God, Knowledge of Man (with sub-sections a–f), Knowledge of the Universe (with sub-sections a–c) — structure the whole discussion.

## Key Proponents / Origins

- [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]] — the phrasing and the programme implementation are hers. The tripartite division has obvious affinities with the medieval / Christian schema of God–humanity–creation, but Mason's interest is operational: it gives her programmes a complete coverage check.
