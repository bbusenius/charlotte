# Visual Spelling and Prepared Dictation

## Definition

In [[entities/charlotte-mason|Charlotte Mason]]'s early-years language-arts method, spelling is taught chiefly by training the eye to retain the correct visual form of a word, then using prepared dictation so the child writes only after seeing and fixing the correct form. The method avoids letting a misspelling remain before the child's eye, because Mason treats the seen image of a wrong spelling as pedagogically harmful.

## Core Ideas

- **Spelling depends on visual memory.** Mason's "rationale of spelling" is that the power to spell depends on the eye's ability to take and retain a detailed picture of a word.
- **The correct form must be seen first.** A child studies the word before writing it, often by visualising it with the eyes shut.
- **Prepared dictation protects the eye from wrong forms.** Doubtful words are shown, studied, and removed before dictation. If a child misspells a word, the mistake is covered immediately so the wrong image is not reinforced.
- **Dictation grows out of real reading.** The words come from prose worth reading, not from isolated arbitrary lists.
- **Phonics has a place, but not as pure drill.** In the reading chapters, Mason uses loose-word boxes and phonic word-building on alternate days, explicitly adapting method to the irregularities of English.

## In Practice

In a Mason lesson, a passage is chosen in advance. Words likely to trouble the child are noticed before dictation, written clearly, looked at, visualised, and then removed. The child writes from dictation only after that preparation. Errors are not left exposed for correction-drill; they are hidden at once and re-taught from the correct form.

For early reading, _Home Education_ pairs whole-word recognition with phonic word-building. The child meets actual words in a meaningful passage, then works with their sounds and letter patterns through loose-word and word-building exercises.

## Variants & Related Concepts

- [[concepts/living-books|Living Books]] — the dictation matter comes from real prose, not from pre-digested exercises.
- [[concepts/narration|Narration]] — composition develops from telling back; spelling and transcription are mechanical foundations, not substitutes for narration.
- [[concepts/single-reading|Single Reading]] — the same discipline of attention underlies prepared dictation: look closely, once, with the expectation of use.
